Measurement · Building 62

Low-Temperature Magneto-Optical Microscope

attocube attoDRY2100

Our attocube attoDRY2100 closed-cycle cryostat supports confocal optical spectroscopy and microscopy from room temperature down to 1.65 K, with a 9 T superconducting magnet (0.1% field homogeneity over a 10 mm sphere). The top-loading design allows interchangeable measurement inserts, and an optical breadboard mounted directly on the cryostat provides free-space optical access. Low vibration makes the system suitable for spatial mapping at cryogenic temperatures.

  • 1.65 K to room temperature: closed-cycle, no liquid helium
  • 9 T superconducting magnet: 0.1% homogeneity over a 10 mm sphere
  • Top-loading inserts: interchangeable, including an RF and strain insert

In development

Cryogenic strain cell

A Razorbill Instruments UC200 strain cell is being integrated into the RF and strain insert to apply controlled tensile and compressive strain to samples at cryogenic temperatures. Built-in sensors independently measure the applied force and displacement, so strain can be both tuned and quantified during an optical measurement. The cell can strain 2D flakes on cleaved silicon chips or on flexible substrates.

For our system, the cell has been lightened to 80 g and will sit on top of the attocube positioner and scanner stack. This keeps the sample within the objective’s working distance and at the center of the 9 T magnet while allowing spatial mapping under strain. Interchangeable sample carriers will let users mount and wire up to four samples before a cooldown. Combined with the RF lines and magnetic field, the strain cell will enable studies of how strain tunes excitons, spin states and phase transitions in quantum materials.

Sample fabrication

Micromechanical Stackers

Our micromechanical stackers assemble van der Waals heterostructures layer by layer: picking up exfoliated flakes of graphene, hBN, transition metal dichalcogenides and other 2D materials, aligning them, and building clean, twist-controlled stacks ready for spectroscopy and microscopy. We run three:

  • Glovebox stacker: assembly under inert atmosphere for air-sensitive materials
  • Ambient stacker: everyday assembly of air-stable heterostructures
  • Robotic stacker: a robot arm in a nitrogen box with tunable humidity, for experimenting with physical AI in sample fabrication
