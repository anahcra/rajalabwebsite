Sample fabrication

Micromechanical Stackers

Our micromechanical stackers assemble van der Waals heterostructures layer by layer: picking up exfoliated flakes of graphene, hBN, transition metal dichalcogenides and other 2D materials, aligning them, and building clean, twist-controlled stacks ready for spectroscopy and microscopy. We run three:

  • Glovebox stacker Building 66Assembly under inert atmosphere for air-sensitive materials
  • Ambient stacker Building 67Everyday assembly of air-stable heterostructures
  • Robotic stacker Building 67A robot arm in a nitrogen box with tunable humidity, for experimenting with physical AI in sample fabrication

Measurement · Building 67

Cryogenic Optical Microscope

Montana Instruments Cryostation s100

Our Montana Instruments Cryostation s100 is a closed-cycle, cryogen-free optical cryostat for spectroscopy and microscopy from 4 K to 350 K. An agile temperature sample mount controls the sample temperature independently of the cold platform, which allows fast temperature-dependent measurements with sub-millikelvin stability. A vacuum-compatible 100x, 0.9 NA objective sits inside the cryostat on a temperature-controlled mount, giving diffraction-limited collection with low drift at cryogenic temperatures.

  • 4 K to 350 K: closed-cycle, cryogen-free
  • Sub-millikelvin stability: sample temperature set independently of the cold platform
  • 100x, 0.9 NA objective: inside the cryostat, diffraction-limited

Sample environment

Closed-loop nanopositioners provide 5 mm of travel in x, y and z, and a piezo scanner covers 40 µm × 40 µm at base temperature for hyperspectral mapping. Samples can be mounted on a 16-pad chip carrier for wire-bonded devices, which supports gate-dependent optical measurements on 2D heterostructures. The carrier uses a quick-change mount, so wire-bonded devices can move between experiments without being rewired.

Measurement capabilities

The microscope combines several excitation sources in a shared optical path: a 532 nm continuous-wave laser for photoluminescence and Raman spectroscopy, including low-frequency Raman; a broadband white light source for reflectance contrast; a pulsed supercontinuum laser for time-resolved measurements; and a tunable ultrafast Ti:Sapphire laser. Emission is analyzed with a spectrometer and CCD, a time-correlated single-photon counting detector for lifetime measurements, and an sCMOS camera that images exciton diffusion. Tunable edge filters isolate specific spectral features. Photoluminescence, Raman and reflectance spectra can be collected as spatial maps, and the full system is automated through ScopeFoundry.

Measurement · Building 62

Low-Temperature Magneto-Optical Microscope

attocube attoDRY2100

Our attocube attoDRY2100 closed-cycle cryostat supports confocal optical spectroscopy and microscopy from room temperature down to 1.65 K, with a 9 T superconducting magnet (0.1% field homogeneity over a 10 mm sphere). The top-loading design allows interchangeable measurement inserts, and an optical breadboard mounted directly on the cryostat provides free-space optical access. Low vibration makes the system suitable for spatial mapping at cryogenic temperatures.

  • 1.65 K to room temperature: closed-cycle, no liquid helium
  • 9 T superconducting magnet: 0.1% homogeneity over a 10 mm sphere
  • Top-loading inserts: interchangeable, including an RF and strain insert

In development

Cryogenic strain cell

A Razorbill Instruments UC200 strain cell is being integrated into the RF and strain insert to apply controlled tensile and compressive strain to samples at cryogenic temperatures. Built-in sensors independently measure the applied force and displacement, so strain can be both tuned and quantified during an optical measurement. The cell can strain 2D flakes on cleaved silicon chips or on flexible substrates.

For our system, the cell has been lightened to 80 g and will sit on top of the attocube positioner and scanner stack. This keeps the sample within the objective’s working distance and at the center of the 9 T magnet while allowing spatial mapping under strain. Interchangeable sample carriers will let users mount and wire up to four samples before a cooldown. Combined with the RF lines and magnetic field, the strain cell will enable studies of how strain tunes excitons, spin states and phase transitions in quantum materials.

Upcoming capabilities · Building 66

Tunable Fiber Microcavity

Our Qlibri system is a cryogenic open microcavity formed between a mirrored sample substrate and a curved mirror on an optical fiber tip. The cavity length and position are tunable and actively locked, so a single system can couple to emitters at different wavelengths and locations without fabricating custom nanocavities. Capabilities will include:

  • Purcell enhancement and direct fiber collection of single photons from quantum emitters
  • Resonant excitation with a broadband, narrow-linewidth tunable laser
  • Detection with broadband superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors (SNSPDs), a CCD and avalanche photodiodes (APDs)
  • Correlation electronics for time-resolved and photon-statistics measurements
  • Measurement of lifetime, linewidth, spectral stability, single-photon purity, coherence, two-photon indistinguishability and entanglement visibility
  • High-throughput screening of color centers in 2D materials, silicon and silicon carbide, f-element molecules and nanoparticles, and Rydberg excitons in Cu₂O

Interfacing with trapped-ion networks

In partnership with Berkeley Lab’s QUANT-NET quantum network testbed, which links trapped-ion quantum computers over fiber, the cavity will benchmark solid-state emitters using the same metrics as ion and atom photonic qubits. This identifies emitters suited to interface with ion and atom network nodes, a step toward heterogeneous quantum networks.
