Raja Labs

Nanoscale Spectroscopy

Molecular Foundry  ·  Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory

Light, electrons & atomically thin matter

We image and time-resolve excitons, phonons, and charge at the interfaces of two-dimensional heterostructures, down to the sub-nanometer and femtosecond scales.

Our work combines ultrafast optical spectroscopy, electron energy-loss and cathodoluminescence microscopy, and ultrafast electron diffraction to reveal how dielectric environment, moiré potentials, and non-equilibrium phonons shape the physics of van der Waals materials.

What we study

Research

μ

Moiré Excitons & Sub-nm Imaging

Hyperspectral imaging of excitons within a single moiré unit cell using correlated optical and low-loss electron energy-loss spectroscopy.

ε

Dielectric Screening in 2D

Coulomb engineering of bandgaps, dielectric disorder, and the role of screening in exciton–phonon scattering and energy transfer.

τ

Ultrafast Energy & Charge Flow

Ultrafast electron diffraction reveals non-equilibrium phonons and electron–phonon energy transport unique to 2D interfaces.

ħω

Heterostructure Design

Engineering interlayer hybridization, hBN encapsulation, and cathodoluminescence of buried interfaces by nanoscale design.

Principal Investigator

Archana Raja

Staff Scientist, Molecular Foundry · Faculty, Kavli Energy NanoScience Institute · LBNL

Archana is an experimental physical chemist studying light–matter interactions in low-dimensional materials. She received her PhD in Chemical Physics from Columbia University with Tony Heinz and Louis Brus, and was a postdoctoral fellow at Stanford and in the Alivisatos group at UC Berkeley before joining the Molecular Foundry in 2019.

Honors · LBNL Director's Award for Exceptional Early Scientific Career (2023) · Early Career LDRD Award (2020–22) · Heising-Simons Junior Fellowship, Kavli ENSI (2017–19) · IIT Bombay Institute Silver Medal
