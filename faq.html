Joining the lab

Are you hiring?

We’re always glad to hear from motivated postdocs, graduate students and undergraduates. Open positions depend on funding, so the best first step is to email Archana. Formal postdoc postings appear on the Berkeley Lab careers site ↗ (opens in a new tab).

What should I include when I email?

Your CV, a few sentences on the research you’ve done and what draws you to our work, and when you’d be available. Specific is better than general: tell us which of our projects or papers caught your eye.

I’m a PhD student at another university. Can I do research here?

Yes. The DOE Office of Science Graduate Student Research (SCGSR) ↗ (opens in a new tab) program supports graduate students to spend part of their PhD at a national lab. Reach out before the application deadline so we can talk about a project together.

Are there opportunities for undergraduates?

Yes. Undergraduates work in the lab on real experiments alongside postdocs. Berkeley Lab also runs paid summer programs such as DOE SULI ↗ (opens in a new tab); see Berkeley Lab education programs ↗ (opens in a new tab) for the full list.

Using our instruments

Can I use your instruments without joining the group?

Yes, through the Molecular Foundry user program ↗ (opens in a new tab). Researchers from universities, labs and industry submit a short proposal; access is free for researchers who intend to publish. Our Facilities page describes what’s available.

Should I contact you before submitting a user proposal?

Please do. A quick conversation helps us check that the measurement is feasible on our setups and makes for a stronger proposal. Email Archana with a short description of your samples and what you want to learn.

Visiting

How do I get on site?

You’ll need a Berkeley Lab badge, or a visitor pass plus a REAL ID. Passes can take several weeks, so get in touch well ahead of your visit. Our Visit page covers the shuttle, badge office and parking.

Where exactly is the lab?

Our labs are in Buildings 67 (the Molecular Foundry), 66 and 62 at Berkeley Lab, 1 Cyclotron Road. The Visit page has a map.
